## Killer Playbook: Stealth, Stalking, and Decoy Kills

When playing the killer, raw aggression rarely yields consistent wins against experienced lobbies. Charging through central halls with your weapon out announces your presence and invites a swift bullet from a hidden Sheriff. High-level execution requires misdirection and tactical patience.

    |                   |

    |                   |

### The Art of the Bait

Experienced players frequently bait killers into overcommitting in long hallways where defenders hold clean sightlines. Counter this behavior by feigning an aggressive rush toward a lone target, then abruptly breaking line of sight around a corner. In many instances, the defender panics and discharges their firearm into the empty wall. 

Community reports emphasize that this cooldown period is your absolute best window to counterattack. Use their reload downtime to close the distance without taking damage.

### Mastering Knife Throws and Movement Perks

Melee strikes are reliable in narrow spaces, but ranged throws secure eliminations against fleeing runners. Avoid throwing at players who are actively dodging in open plazas. Instead, line up your throws down tight corridors, doorways, or stairwells where target movement is restricted.

| Killer Perk | Playstyle Alignment | Tactical Advantage | Primary Counter |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ninja** | Stealth / Infiltration | Eliminates sound cues during takedowns | Visual spotters and grouped players |
| **Haste** | Aggressive Chaser | Grants sudden movement bursts to close gaps | Narrow choke points and defensive camp spots |
| **X-Ray / Tracking** | Information / Endgame | Locates hidden survivors camping in odd corners | Constant survivor repositioning |
| **Trap / Decoy** | Defensive Area Control | Cuts off primary evacuation routes | Players avoiding cramped rooms |

A well-rounded **killer or innocent strategy** treats perks not as crutches, but as tools to amplify map control. If you choose the Ninja perk, prioritize picks on isolated players in side rooms before the team realizes the player count is dropping.

## Innocent Playbook: Survival, Sightlines, and Intel

Surviving without a weapon requires awareness and spatial discipline. You are not helpless; you are the eyes and ears of the team, and in many modes, the immediate backup shooter should the primary defender fall.

### Situational Positioning Rules

1. **Avoid Dead Ends:** Never enter small, single-door storage closets or dead-end bathrooms unless you have verified the killer's exact position on the opposite side of the map.
2. **Observe Elimination Feeds and Audio:** Keep a mental tally of where players disappear. If three people walk into the library and only one walks out, you have your suspect.
3. **Scatter on Sight:** When the weapon is drawn, never run in a straight line behind another player. Clustered groups grant the killer collateral melee hits or lucky throws.

| Scenario | Incorrect Reaction | Recommended Tactical Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Killer pulls weapon nearby** | Running in a straight hallway | Serpentine jumping through doorways or around clutter |
| **Sheriff drops the weapon** | Immediately rushing out in the open | Verifying killer position, then smoking/clearing before picking it up |
| **Trapped in a room** | Freezing in a far corner | Timing a jump over the killer's swing to reach the doorway |
| **Suspicious player follows you** | Ignoring them while collecting coins | Rotating around obstacles to keep them behind physical cover |

### The Innocent Support Dynamic

A critical aspect of an effective **killer or innocent strategy** is weapon recovery. When the primary protector dies, their dropped weapon becomes the objective. Do not sprint directly toward the dropped weapon immediately. Experienced killers camp the dropped gun from elevated positions or behind adjacent doors, waiting for desperate innocents to run straight into a knife throw. 

Instead, circle the perimeter, verify the killer's line of sight, and make an explosive approach only when the killer is distracted by another survivor.

## Advanced 1v1 Showdowns and Mechanical Outplays

Inevitably, rounds distill down to a tense final duel between the killer and the last remaining armed player. These high-stress encounters demand mechanical precision and emotional control.

    |           |

### Elevation and Environment Control

Jumping from balconies, sliding down ramps, and leaping over interior furniture disrupts your opponent's crosshair placement. Player experience reveals that killers who leverage verticality to drop directly onto defenders win engagements significantly faster than those who approach at eye level. 

Conversely, armed defenders should maintain mid-range distance, positioning themselves near obstacles that block incoming projectiles while preserving a wide field of view.

### The "Trust Fall" Defensive Method

When facing a killer sprinting directly toward you, human instinct urges you to fire immediately. However, panic shooting leads to missed shots and fatal reloads. Incorporate a defensive pause: let the killer execute their initial zig-zag approach, predict the point where they must commit to a straight line for the strike, and take your shot. Remaining composed when under pressure separates top-tier defenders from beginner players.

## Step-by-Step Tactical Round Progression

To consistently win matches, break each round into distinct phases with clear checklists for both sides:

```
[ Early Round: Dispersal ]
Gather intel, avoid crowds, spot role reveals.
          ↓
[ Mid Round: Elimination & Clues ]
Track body drops, watch dropped gun, rotate lanes.
          ↓
[ Late Round: The Endgame 1v1 ]
Secure weapon, control sightlines, land the final hit.
```

### Early Phase (First 30 Seconds)
- **Killer:** Do not equip your weapon immediately. Observe player clusters, locate the Sheriff, and plan an initial silent takedown away from onlookers.
- **Innocent / Defender:** Spread out across wide hallways. Keep a safe distance from anyone tailing your movement, and establish clear line-of-sight exits.

### Mid Phase (30 to 90 Seconds)
- **Killer:** Target isolated players. Avoid dragging bodies into main lobbies. If seen, swiftly eliminate the witness before they report your identity.
- **Innocent / Defender:** Check social feeds or callouts. If you carry the weapon, hold central lanes where you can protect multiple survivors without cornering yourself.

### Late Phase (Endgame Duels)
- **Killer:** Fake aggressive pushes to exhaust the defender's shots. Use horizontal jumps to evade incoming bullets.
- **Innocent / Defender:** Maintain your distance. Never fire until the target commits to an irreversible movement animation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the most effective killer or innocent strategy for beginners?
For beginners, the most effective **killer or innocent strategy** focuses entirely on positioning and map awareness. As an innocent, avoid dead-end rooms and never trail directly behind players you cannot verify. As a killer, keep your weapon holstered until you isolate a single target in an empty corridor, preventing early lobby callouts.

### How do I counter a killer who camps the dropped weapon?
Never sprint in a straight line toward a dropped weapon. Circle the area to spot hiding spots, crates, or high ledges where the killer might be waiting. Coordinate with other survivors to distract the killer, or use sudden jumping routes and corner slides to grab the gun and fire instantly.

### When should a defender hold their fire during a chase?
A defender should hold their fire until the killer moves into an open space or commits to a straight-line attack. Firing out of fear through doorways or around sharp corners often misses, giving the killer a free takedown during your weapon recovery frames. Always confirm the weapon is visible before pulling the trigger.
