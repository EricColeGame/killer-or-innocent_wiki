## Core Roles and Win Conditions

Every social deduction round creates an asymmetrical conflict between predator and prey. Understanding each faction's win conditions and mechanical capabilities is fundamental before diving into advanced execution.

| Role | Primary Win Condition | Primary Equipment | Threat Level | Primary Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Innocent** | Outlast the timer or pick up dropped weapon to eliminate the threat | None (initially) | Low (Passive) | Corner traps, ambush paths |

### The Flow of a Match

A typical round breaks down into three distinct phases:

1. **The Gathering Phase (0:00 – 0:30):** Players spread across the map, collect resources, and scout behaviors. The killer looks for isolated stragglers.
2. **The Escalation Phase (0:30 – 1:30):** The first bodies drop. Innocents realize the killer's identity, and the armed protector attempts to set up an ambush line.
3. **The Endgame (1:30+ or Post-Drop):** If the protector falls, the dropped firearm becomes a volatile capture point where remaining innocents must choose between fleeing or fighting back.

## Killer Mastery: Stealth, Ambush, and Knife Mechanics

Playing the aggressor is not about charging blindly into corridors. High-tier killers rely on tempo control, fake disinterest, and sharp execution. The following **killer or innocent tips** highlight how killers can dismantle teams methodically.

| Killer Playstyle | Primary Advantage | Main Weakness | Recommended Map Types |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Silent Stalker** | Keeps innocents guessing; delays panic | Slower match pacing | Multi-room, labyrinthine layouts |
| **Aggressive Rusher** | Overwhelms players before they communicate | Leaves you open to long-range shots | Tight corridors, small indoor spaces |
| **Drop Camper** | Forces armed heroes into predictable paths | Vulnerable to flankers | Wide-open central lobbies |

### 1. The Art of the Sheathed Blade

Never walk with your weapon drawn unless you are mid-swing or aiming a throw. Keeping your blade unequipped lets you blend into crowds, sprint alongside unsuspecting groups, and slip into rooms without raising immediate alarms. Drawing the blade right behind an opponent provides zero reaction window.

### 2. Mastering the Throwing Arc

Relying purely on melee attacks makes you an easy target for any competent shooter. When engaging at medium range:

- **Lead Your Target:** Players rarely move in a straight line once alerted. Predict their path toward doorways.
- **Shoulder-Peeking:** Step out from behind a corner, throw your projectile, and step back instantly to break the shooter's line of sight.
- **Jump-Throw Variations:** Jumping alters your vertical hit trajectory, making it harder for an armed survivor to headshot or center-mass snipe you during your windup.

### 3. Controlling the Fallen Weapon

When you eliminate the armed protector, their weapon drops onto the floor, emitting a bright glow. Do not stand directly on top of it. Camping directly over the item alerts everyone to your location and lets skilled survivors snipe you as they round the corner. Instead, hold an elevated position or hide around a blind corner nearby, striking as soon as an innocent commits to the pickup animation.

## Innocent Survival: Positioning, Pathing, and Evasion

Playing as an unarmed survivor feels powerless only if you move without purpose. Surviving requires acute audio awareness, constant escape-route planning, and deliberate body language.

| Survival Habit | Why It Works | Common Execution Mistake |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dead-End Avoidance** | Prevents killers from cornering you without counterplay | Getting trapped while hunting for coins/loot |
| **Line-of-Sight Breaking** | Cancels knife throws and resets chase pursuit | Running in straight lines through open hallways |
| **Shadowing the Gunner** | Keeps you close to protection and weapon drop | Standing so close that one swing hits both players |
| **Erratic Bunny-Hopping** | Throws off projectile prediction | Depleting momentum into wall edges |

### Reading Suspicious Behavior

Killers constantly give away their intent before they strike. Watching how players navigate rooms reveals their role:

- **Aggressive Cutting:** Innocents wander casually or follow resource lines. A killer cuts angles to intercept your path.
- **The Staring Contest:** If a player stops moving and rotates their camera toward you without collecting nearby objectives, they are calculating throw distance.
- **Backtracking into Quiet Rooms:** Killers regularly scout side rooms to confirm whether targets are alone.

These fundamental **killer or innocent tips** ensure that even without a weapon, you waste the killer's precious time, giving your armed teammates an opportunity to line up the winning shot.

## The Dropped Gun Scenario: High-Risk Decision Making

The climax of nearly every round occurs when the protector is eliminated, leaving their firearm on the ground. Deciding whether to retrieve it or run determines the match outcome.

```

    distract the killer.       (Yes)          (No)
                              /                  \

                    slide/jump; retrieve   grab weapon, and
                    and turn-shoot.       hold sightline.
```

### Protocol for Weapon Recovery

1. **Survey Sightlines First:** Spend one second checking high perches and tight corners before dashing toward the beam of light.
2. **Never Run in a Straight Line:** Approach the drop using zig-zag movement or slide mechanics to avoid being pinned by a mid-air knife.
3. **Pre-Aim During Recovery:** The moment your character touches the firearm, snap your camera toward the direction the killer last occupied. Most killers lunge forward right as you bend down.

| Map Layout Archetype | Killer Strategy | Innocent Strategy | Best Weapon Strategy |

### Using Third-Person Camera Angles

Both killers and survivors should utilize third-person camera rotation around corners. By angling your viewpoint against doorframes, you can inspect adjacent rooms without exposing your character model. Community reports frequently emphasize that players who rely solely on first-person view miss flanking attempts and sneak attacks.

## Psychological Mind Games and Social Bluffing

Mechanical skill wins duels, but psychological dominance wins matches. Use these behavioral **killer or innocent tips** to manipulate lobby dynamics:

- **The Hesitation Bait:** When unarmed, run toward a dropped firearm for two paces, then violently reverse direction. Killers often throw their knife anticipating your grab, leaving them weaponless for several seconds during cooldown.
- **Innocent Mimicry:** As a killer, pick up map items and jump around playfully with other survivors early in the round. Building false camaraderie prevents panic until you isolate a target.
- **Split-Second Freezing:** If a killer spots you across a long corridor, abruptly stopping behind cover rather than continuing your run often causes them to check the wrong doorway.

Applying these balanced **killer or innocent tips** across every session builds the situational instinct needed to carry lobbies consistently.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the most important killer or innocent tips for beginners?
Focus on spatial awareness and audio cues. If you are an innocent, never corner yourself in rooms with a single exit, and never walk in straight lines. If you are a killer, keep your weapon hidden until the moment of attack to prevent early lobby callouts.

### How do I practice knife throwing accuracy?
Warm up by predicting player movement through doorways rather than aiming directly at their current position. Throwing where an opponent will be in one second—especially while they are committed to jumping or running through a narrow hallway—yields far higher hit rates than attempting reactive flick throws in open spaces.

### Should innocents group together or scatter across the map?
Scattering is generally much safer. While grouping offers comfort, a skilled killer can eliminate three to four grouped innocents in seconds before anyone can react. Maintaining medium distance from other survivors allows you to observe who attacks whom without becoming collateral damage.

### What should I do if the killer is camping the dropped weapon?
Do not rush in solo. Wait for another survivor to draw the killer's focus or attempt an entry from an alternate angle. If the killer refuses to leave the weapon, use high-ground peeks or coordinated distractions to force them to commit to an attack before you attempt to retrieve the firearm.
