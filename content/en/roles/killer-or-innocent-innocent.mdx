### The 3-Phase Survival Protocol

1. **Early Game (0:00 - 1:30):** Stick to open corridors and high-visibility rooms. Complete immediate, quick-action tasks before major sabotages can be coordinated.
2. **Mid Game (1:30 - 3:30):** Form a soft alliance with one verified player. Community reports suggest that maintaining line-of-sight with at least one witness drops your death probability by nearly 65%.
3. **Late Game (3:30+):** When numbers dwindle, do not isolate yourself to finish minor tasks. Group up, verify remaining tasks, and watch physical exits carefully.

## The Killer Playbook: Stealth, Manipulation, and Framing

Securing victory as a killer is an exercise in applied psychology. You must avoid being caught red-handed while subtly nudging innocent players into accusing each other. In high-level lobbies, players quickly spot obvious lies; your alibis must be technically true, even if fundamentally misleading.

### Establishing the Perfect Alibi

An effective alibi relies on three distinct elements:
- **Proximity:** Being seen near active players seconds before an elimination occurs elsewhere.
- **Task Mimicry:** Standing at real objective nodes for the exact duration of the task.
- **Narrative Anchoring:** Providing the initial, credible location report during meetings before innocents establish the timeline.

| Deception Tactic | Execution Difficulty | Detection Risk | Ideal Game Stage | Expected Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Third-Party Framing** | High | Low | Mid Game | Forces innocents to vote out their own team |
| **Ghost Ambush** | Extreme | Medium | Late Game | Secures final kill under blackout or sabotage |
| **Silent Drifting** | Low | Very Low | Throughout | Keeps killer off suspect lists entirely |

Player experience demonstrates that the most successful killers do not aggressively lead votes. Instead, they act as cooperative investigators, asking questions that gently highlight discrepancies in another innocent player's route. By muddying the waters around who is a true **killer or innocent innocent**, the predator stays invisible while the herd turns on itself.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the most reliable way to tell if someone is a killer or innocent innocent?
Look for contradictions in movement timing rather than general demeanor. Killers often make mistakes when detailing how long a specific task took or which route they used to arrive at a crime scene. Cross-referencing their story with another player's timeline is the most reliable deduction method.

### Should an innocent player ever lie during an emergency meeting?
Generally, no. Innocents who lie—even to protect an investigative ability or test a theory—create confusion that killers exploit. Transparent, accurate data from every innocent player makes it far easier to isolate the killer's inconsistencies.

### What should you do as a killer if someone sees you eliminate a target?
You have two primary options: eliminate the witness before they can report or hit an emergency button, or self-report immediately and aggressively accuse the witness of the kill. The second option relies heavily on your credibility and standing within the lobby.

### How do experienced players handle a killer or innocent innocent tie-breaker vote?
In high-stakes tie-breaker situations, unless a witness can provide verifiable proof (such as visual task confirmation), competitive lobbies typically skip the vote. Forcing a 50/50 guess when numbers are low often hands the opposing faction an instant win.
